## Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra

<a href="http://www-math.mit.edu/~gs/">![](http://habrastorage.org/files/a2b/4d7/a13/a2b4d7a131ab4a58a4b3dcae1b0a930a.jpg)</a>

This is a series of articles devoted to Gilbert Strang's Paper "The fundamental theorem of linear algebra." published by American Mathematical Monthly in 1993 ([pdf](http://www.engineering.iastate.edu/~julied/classes/CE570/Notes/strangpaper.pdf)).


The paper describes the "Strang's diagram", a diagram that shows
actions of $A$, an $m \times n$ matrix, as linear transformations
from the space $\mathbb R^m$ to $\mathbb R^n$. The diagram helps to
understand the fundamental concepts of Linear Algebra in terms of the
four subspaces by visually illustrating the actions of $A$ on all
these subspaces.

In this series of blog posts we will work through the paper and will try to understand it.

![](http://alexeygrigorev.com/projects/imsem-ws14-lina/img-svg/diagram1.svg)

There are 4 parts of the Fundamental theorem of Linear Algebra:

1. The dimensions of the subspaces;
2. The orthogonality of the subspaces;
3. The basis vectors are orthogonal;
4. The matrix with respect to these bases is orthogonal.

And there are four blog posts based on materials from the paper:

1. [The Four Fundamental Subspaces](http://www.itshared.org/2015/04/the-four-fundamental-subspaces.html) (theorem parts 1 and 2)
2. [The Least Squares: Projection onto Subspaces](Projection_onto_Subspaces) (theorem parts 1 and 2)
3. Singular Value Decomposition (theorem parts 3 and 4) (To be added)
4. Pseudoinverse (theorem parts 3 and 4) (To be added)
